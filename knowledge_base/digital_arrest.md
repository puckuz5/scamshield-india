## How it works
The caller pretends to be from the CBI, ED, police, a cyber cell, customs or even a court. They say that you, or someone in your family, is linked to a serious crime such as drug trafficking or money laundering. A common opening is a "parcel in your name" that was supposedly seized with drugs or fake passports.

The victim is then told to stay on a video call (often WhatsApp or Skype), keep the camera on, not leave the house and not tell anyone. This is the so-called "digital arrest". The scammer finally demands money to "verify" funds, pay "bail" or clear the case. The money goes to accounts the scammer controls.

## The fact that breaks the scam
The Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) has made it clear that the CBI, police, customs, ED and judges do **not** arrest anyone over a video call. There is no legal procedure called "digital arrest".

## Red flags
- An officer contacts you on WhatsApp, Skype or a video call
- Threats of immediate arrest unless you pay or "verify" money
- Being told to stay on camera, stay isolated, or keep it secret from family
- Fake ID cards, uniforms, police-station backgrounds or official-looking letters shown on video
- Being asked to move money to a "safe" or "RBI verification" account

## What to do
Stay calm and hang up. Do not transfer money. Talk to a family member. If you already paid, call the national cybercrime helpline **1930** immediately (speed matters for freezing money) and file a complaint at **cybercrime.gov.in**.
