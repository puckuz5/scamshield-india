## How it works
The scammer pretends to send you money: a buyer on an online marketplace, someone who "paid by mistake", or a refund agent. They then send a QR code to scan or a payment request to approve, and say you need to enter your UPI PIN to receive the money. Entering the PIN actually sends money from your account. A related trick is the "jumped deposit": a small amount is credited, followed by a request to "return" a larger one.

## The facts that break the scam
- NPCI's safety guidance: the UPI PIN is needed **only to send money**, never to receive it.
- QR codes are scanned to **pay**, not to receive money.
- NPCI discontinued person-to-person (P2P) UPI collect requests from **1 October 2025**. Only merchants can still send collect requests, so a "collect request" from an individual is itself a warning sign.

## Red flags
- "Enter your PIN to receive the money"
- "Scan this QR code to get your refund / payment"
- A buyer who claims to be in the army or defence and insists on UPI
- "I sent extra money by mistake, please return it" right after an unexpected credit
- Pressure to approve a request quickly

## What to do
Never enter your UPI PIN to receive money. Check the receiver's name before paying. For unexpected credits, check your bank app directly and wait; do not "return" money via a link or request. If money was lost, call **1930** and your bank immediately.
